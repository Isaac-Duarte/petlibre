# Wall clock, offline time and missed-time policy (FW21, FW46)

Status: FW21's sync and jump classification are physical (October 22). FW46's error bound, drift learning, bounded steps, zone setting and NTP client are host-tested and simulated (October 26); the zone across reboots, a first NTP fix and the RTC across warm resets are physical (October 26, v232), and the rate bounds and the warm-reset carry that this evidence led to are host-tested, with their bench run [below](#bench-plan). Code: `wellfed-core/src/clock/` (`wall.rs`, `zone.rs`, `ntp.rs`), `wellfed-hal/src/clock.rs`, `wellfed-feeder/src/feeder/time.rs` and `src/store/time.rs`, `wellfed-feeder-runtime/src/time.rs`, and `wellfed-client/src/time.rs` for the rule a client sends.

## Sources

- **A client's sync** (`Op::TimeSync`, inside a Noise session): the time, the zone's offset now and its next change. The `wellfed time` command sends this computer's clock and zone.
- **NTP** from the loop (below), when the time settings name a server. Offline means no server and no cloud, not no network: the default server is the DHCP gateway, which many home routers answer for.

A fix is a time and how far off it could be: a client sync counts 1 s for its trip across the LAN; an NTP answer half its round trip less the server's processing, plus the server's root delay and dispersion, plus 3 ms of rounding.

## The clock

- **Invalid after a power-on.** A warm reset (watchdog or software: a restart, an activation, a trial boot) resumes the clock from the RTC (see [warm resets](#warm-resets)); a power-on, a brownout or a reset that finds nothing to resume starts unsynced.
- **Carried by uptime** between fixes, corrected by the learned rate.
- **The trusted interval.** Only a trusted fix sets the clock's anchor: an owner's sync, or any fix while the clock is invalid (the first, or after expiry). From the anchor, the true time lies within the anchor's error plus the rate bound times the time since.
- **The rate bounds, from the bench.** The uptime clock ran 735 ppm slow against the server (two 10-minute resyncs, physical, October 22) and 2,060 ppm slow against the host's timesyncd-kept clock (one 1,026 s NTP poll, physical, October 26, another build); the 2,000 ppm bound of the time refused that poll. What makes the rate move by a factor of three is not known (the tick's source, load, temperature). So:
  - **Unlearned: 5,000 ppm,** more than twice the worst measured. The price is the validity window, 2 minutes over the bound: about 6.6 h after a trusted fix (it was 16.5 h at 2,000 ppm).
  - **Learned floor: 1,500 ppm,** above the 1,325 ppm the measured rate moved between the two measurements: a learned correction is not trusted any tighter than the hardware has been seen to vary. That gives about 22 h of validity after a trusted fix (it was 7 to 14 days at 100 ppm). A bench run that logs the rate across a long uptime can lower it.
  - **A bad fix:** a measured rate past ±10,000 ppm (1 %).
- **The cadence this gives.** The clock holds about **6.6 h** after a trusted fix while the rate is unlearned and about **22 h** once an owner's syncs have taught it, so in practice it is re-set at least daily:
  - **With NTP** (the gateway by default) nothing is needed: polls every 17 minutes keep the reading, and at each expiry the next answer (within 64 s) re-sets it inside the old interval. The clock reads invalid for up to a minute each time.
  - **Without NTP,** something must sync it: the app or the server at least every ~6 h until an owner's syncs have taught the rate, then at least daily. A home without a router that answers NTP can set the server to a public pool's address (`wellfed time --ntp IP[:PORT]`; hostnames are not resolved yet). Product: the app and the server should sync on every connection and warn when the time view says the clock needs the time.
- **Drift learning,** from owners' syncs only, at both ends. An owner's sync is measured against a baseline (the first owner's sync since the rate was last forgotten); once it measures the rate to within 50 ppm, the correction applies and the bound drops to the floor. A later measurement that disagrees widens the bound by the difference. An owner's sync that disagrees with the clock forgets the learned rate. The rate is not stored or carried across a reset; a boot learns it again. With owner syncs a few tens of minutes apart (NTP-like errors) it is learned within the window; owner syncs alone (a client's one second each) need about 11 hours, so the second lands after the first window expired and is trusted then. NTP alone never teaches it (below), so an NTP-only clock stays at the unlearned bound, which its polls make no worse than a learned one; a first owner's sync then becomes the baseline and a second one measures the rate. A free NTP set (the first fix after a boot, or after three misses) is never a baseline: an attacker's boot-time answer 100 s off would otherwise let an owner's sync six hours later learn +4,630 ppm with a 1,500 ppm bound and leave the clock 333 s off at 20 h while it claimed 109 s (host-tested).
- **Why not learn from NTP fixes inside the interval.** They would teach the rate sooner, but a fix that only had to fall inside the trusted interval can sit anywhere in it, so a rate measured from two of them is uncertain by the interval's width over the span: the unlearned bound again. Such a rate could move the reading but could never tighten the bound, which is all learning is for, and moving the reading is what an in-interval fix already does. So the rate stays learned from owners' syncs only, which also keeps the poisoning the review found closed. The same holds for the NTP answer that re-sets an expired clock (below): it only had to fall inside the expired interval, so it teaches no rate either. Without that, an answerer at the edge each expiry could teach the clock a skewed rate and walk it about 30 minutes in three days while it claimed the learned bound (host-tested).
- **Untrusted fixes** (NTP, or a member's or the server's sync) must fall inside the trusted interval, or they are refused (`Refusal::Time`, `PLTIM1 sync ok=0 error=bounded`, NTP's `bounded`). Inside, the reading moves to the overlap of the two intervals, but its reported error never drops below what the anchor allows: the reading's distance from the anchor's estimate plus the anchor's error. So an untrusted fix can improve where the clock reads, cannot make it claim more than it knows, cannot teach it a rate, and cannot walk it outside the trusted interval. Internally everything is kept in microseconds, so rounding cannot walk it either.
- **Validity.** The clock reads invalid once its reported error passes **2 minutes** (`MAX_ERROR_MS`): about 6.6 h after a trusted fix unlearned (sooner if untrusted fixes moved the reading, which the error counts), about 22 h learned. Then the next fix sets it again, and the NTP exchange asks within 64 s instead of waiting out its poll. An owner's or a client's sync sets it freely. An NTP answer must fall inside the expired anchor's interval, carried to now and grown at the rate bound with no two-minute cap (about ±2 minutes just after expiry); outside, it is refused `bounded` and counts as a miss. So each NTP re-set moves the clock by at most that interval rather than to anything, and an answerer at the edge every expiry walks it about 2 minutes per 6.6 h (host-tested). Only after three misses in a row is the anchor forgotten, and then any answer sets it. Three NTP answers in a row that all miss the trusted interval mean the anchor, the rate or the server is wrong: the clock forgets the anchor and the rate and reads invalid, so the feeder says it needs the time rather than stay silently valid; a member's disagreement does not count.
- **What this bounds.** Host-tested with the review's attacks: an attacker answering NTP hourly at the edge of the clock's interval never makes a valid reading claim less error than it has, and with an owner's sync every 12 hours the clock stays honest for a week; without one it expires (within 7 h) rather than lie; one spoofed answer teaches no rate. What NTP is trusted with is setting an invalid clock within its expired interval, and a clock with no anchor at all (the first fix, or after three misses) to anything: whoever answers the feeder's requests at that moment sets it. An on-path attacker can therefore walk the clock about 2 minutes each expiry, or make it miss three times and then set it freely; an owner's sync, or the app showing the time, is the check. Feeding plans (FP03) that must not run on an unconfirmed time can treat a clock whose last fix was NTP after a `First` (the time view's `last: Ntp`, with no owner sync since) as unconfirmed.
- **Classification:** a fix that moves the clock by at most 2 s is a step, anything else a jump, logged with its signed size and recorded as a `ClockSync` event.
- **Refused fixes** leave the clock unchanged: a time before 2026 or after 2099; for a sync, an offset outside −12 h to +14 h or a next change that is not in the future.

## Time without a source

No valid clock means the feeder needs the time. The device API says so: `Status.clock_ms` is `None`, and the time view's `clock_ms` and `error_ms` are `None`. Scheduled meals hold (there is no scheduler yet; FP03 runs nothing while the clock is invalid, by the missed-meal rule below), and the Dispense button and client feeds still work: no feed path reads the wall clock. Showing it on the panel is FW23's and FP12's.

## Time zone

The zone is a feeder setting (`PutTime`'s `zone`): a POSIX TZ rule such as `MST7MDT,M3.2.0,M11.1.0`, which the feeder evaluates for any year without a zone database (`wellfed_core::clock::zone`). A client turns the owner's IANA name into the rule with `wellfed_client::time::rule`: the footer of the zone's TZif file (RFC 8536) in the tzdb `jiff-tzdb` bundles, checked by the feeder's own parser (offsets −12 h to +14 h, as syncs) and against both the bundled zone and the host's view of it, which the caller reads and passes in, at every transition of the next ten years. A footer only describes the zone after its file's last listed transition, so a zone that lists irregular changes ahead is refused (`RuleError::Irregular`) and stays on the syncs' offsets; in the current tzdb no zone is (host-tested over every zone). Every footer fits the 48 bytes the store keeps.

Without a zone setting, local time comes from the last client sync's offset and next change, as before: correct across one change. A rule wins over a sync's offsets. `wellfed time --zone America/Denver` (or `--zone system`) sets it; `--clear-zone` clears it.

## NTP

`wellfed_core::clock::ntp` is the exchange, sans-IO; `wellfed_feeder::feeder::time` runs it in the loop task on one non-blocking UDP socket, as the mDNS responder does (no new task):

- **Server:** the time settings' `ntp`: `Off`, `Gateway { port }` (the default, port 123: the lease's gateway) or `Address { ip, port }`. The default trusts nothing about the gateway but that it is on the path: an unauthenticated answer from whoever holds the gateway's address and sees the request's nonce. That bounds what it may do to a valid clock (below), not what it may set an invalid one to. Hostnames (`pool.ntp.org`) would need a DNS lookup in the loop; they are not supported yet. Nothing is sent before DHCP gives an address, and a changed server or gateway is asked at once.
- **Requests** carry nothing but a random 64-bit nonce in the transmit timestamp (RFC 9109's data minimization); an answer must come from the server's address and port and echo the nonce, checked first, so a datagram of any other shape leaves the exchange waiting for the real answer; then be a version 3 or 4 server answer, synchronized (leap not 3, stratum 1–15), dated 2026–2099 (NTP era 1 from 2036 is read), and loose by at most 10 s.
- **Pacing:** one request at a time, an answer within 2 s; a lost or unusable answer retries after 8 s, backing off to 64 s; after a good one the next goes 1024 s (about 17 minutes) later, or within 64 s while the clock needs the time. A kiss-o'-death `RATE` holds the exchange for an hour and `DENY` or `RSTR` for a day, and nothing shortens a hold, not even a clock that needs the time (host-tested on the firmware's loop: a denying server is not asked again that day).
- **Records:** `PLAPI1 ntp server=… | socket open=… | answer result=used|bounded|refused error_ms=… | refused reason=… | lost`; the clock itself logs `PLTIM1 sync ok=1 source=ntp kind=first|step|jump delta_ms=… error_ms=… at_ms=…`. A first fix and a jump are recorded in the history as `ClockSync` by `Source::Feeder`; a fix in step is not, so polling does not wear the event ring.

## Time settings

`GetTime` and `PutTime { base, changes }`, behind `Capabilities::TIME` ([device API](device-api.md#evolution)): a new op pair, a new outcome (`Time(TimeView)`) and new enums, appended with their fixtures; nothing existing changed bytes. `GetTime` is anyone's; `PutTime` is an owner's only, since the NTP server decides which fixes the clock hears; it is answered `Done` once the store holds the settings asked for (or already held them), and refused `NotWritable` when the writer could not write them. The bit is 8 (`FEED` 1, `UPDATE` 2, `READINESS` 4): 4 is FW17's, assigned on another branch, so the bits stay unique when both merge. The view carries the settings' generation, whether they are writable, the zone's rule, the server, the clock (`clock_ms`, `error_ms`, `offset_s`), the learned rate and its bound, the sync count, the last fix's source and age, and the last NTP result.

**Storage.** The settings live in a store of their own, storage sectors 27 and 28 (`wellfed_feeder::store::time`): a 128-byte dual-slot record, magic `PLFTIM01`, version 1 (pinned in `store_layout.rs`). The settings writer opens it with the Wi-Fi store and writes it under the write turn and the arming attempt marker, as every store write. The settings record (sectors 2 and 3) is unchanged, and so is its stale `utc_offset_minutes` field. **No bank-0 refresh is needed first:** a build that predates this store never reads or writes sectors 27 and 28 (they were free), so bank 0's build keeps running on its own settings and ignores these, and bank 1 reads defaults until an owner writes them.

## Warm resets

**The RTC (physical, October 26, v232).** The KM0 RTC (`0x48004000`, an always-on BCD calendar on the 32 kHz clock: a 9-bit day count and hours, minutes and seconds) counts seconds from power-on although nothing initializes it (`ISR`'s INITS flag is clear at every boot). It kept counting across watchdog, activation and trial resets (1,613 s against the host's 1,613.1 s) and restarts at 0 on a power cut. The firmware still never writes it, and still logs it read-only at boot (`PLTIM1 rtc tr=… cr=… isr=… prer=… at_ms=…`).

**Where the clock waits (static analysis of the pinned SDK, October 26).** The RTC gives the seconds across a reset; the wall time at a known RTC second and its error must wait somewhere a warm reset keeps and a power cut loses:

- **KM0 backup registers** survive warm resets, but every register the SDK leaves to the user is taken (1: the attempt and update bits and the lease fault; 2: the silent-reset breadcrumb; 3: KM4's boot count; 4: the peer boot count and the trial count; 5: the KM0 heartbeat).
- **Retention SRAM** (1 KiB at `0x000C0000`) is zeroed by KM0's start code on every boot that is not a deep-sleep wake (`rtl8721dlp_app_start.c`).
- **KM4's main RAM** is not cleared by a warm reset in any code the SDK ships: the KM4 boot loader (`boot_flash_hp.c`) clears its own `.bss` in its secure region and copies only the image's RAM data (`.ram_image2.entry`, `.text`, `.data`, the sections the image's `ram_2.bin` holds); `app_start` clears only `.ram_image2.bss`; the FreeRTOS heap (`heap_5`, `CONFIG_DYNAMIC_HEAP_SIZE`) runs from `__bfsram_end__` to `__sram_end__`, the top of `BD_RAM_NS` at `0x1007C000`; the ROM's and boot loader's RAM, the secure stack and the non-secure main stack lie outside it (`0x1007C000` and up, below `0x10005000`). What the ROM does to this RAM on a warm reset cannot be read from the SDK: **bench step 2 decides whether the carry has a home** (a `resumed` after `wellfed reboot`; `empty` or `corrupt` there means it does not, and the clock stays invalid after every reset as before).

So the builder's KM4 linker script takes the top 64 bytes from the heap (`__sram_end__` lowered by 64; `SdkAnchor::SramEnd`) and names them `wellfed_time_carry` (`0x1007BFC0`, the same address in every build, so a record survives an activation into a new build). `example_entry.c` loads and stores the 16 words and cleans the store out of the D-cache (the SDK's header says the cache writes through; the clean holds either way).

**The record** (`WallClock::carry`, in RAM only, so no flash format changes): the RTC second, the trusted estimate and its error and the reading at that second (microseconds), the zone hint, the last fix's origin and age (so the time view's `last` survives, and with it whether an owner confirmed the time), a version and a CRC-32. It is written after every accepted fix and every 10 s from the loop, and cleared while the clock is invalid, so a reset never finds a clock older than the last correction or one that had expired.

**Resuming** (`WallClock::resume`, from the start hook before anything reads the clock; `PLTIM1 carry result=resumed gap_s=… error_ms=… rtc_s=… at_ms=…` or `result=cold|empty|corrupt|rtc|backwards|stale|expired|set`). Only after a watchdog or software reset (the ROM's reset flags; a power-on, brownout or deep-sleep wake is `cold`), from a record that passes its magic, version, CRC and range checks, with an RTC readable as a 24-hour count (two reads that agree), no earlier than the record's second (`backwards`: the RTC restarted), and at most 600 s after it (`stale`). The gap of `g` RTC seconds moves both estimates by `g` s and grows the error by 1 s (both ends are whole RTC seconds) plus `(g + 1)` × 10,000 ppm (the RTC's rate, wide since the gap is short); a grown error past 2 minutes is `expired`. The resumed interval is trusted, as the saved one was (it only grew from a trusted fix), so untrusted fixes still only narrow it; it is no baseline for a rate, and the rate and sync count start again, while the last fix keeps its origin and its age. Whatever came of it, the boot then stores its own record or clears it. Host-tested (`wall_clock.rs`, and through the fake RAM and RTC in `wellfed-hal`'s `clock.rs`).

**What it does not catch.** Every build that resumes clears the record at a cold boot. A build that does not know the record (bank 0's `92c7adec…` today, which uses that RAM as heap top) leaves it alone, so a record could outlive a power cut if the RAM cells held their contents through it, the next boot ran that build, and a later warm reset into this one found the RTC count within 600 s past the record's: then the gap would miss the time before the cut. That needs the RAM to keep 64 bytes through a power loss exactly; once bank 0 runs a build that resumes, it goes away.

## Missed-meal rule (for FP03)

`wall_clock::occurrence(due, evaluated, now)` decides each scheduled occurrence. `evaluated` is a persisted high-water mark of time already evaluated; after each evaluation it becomes `max(evaluated, now)`.

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| No valid clock | Nothing runs |
| `due` after `now` | Later |
| `due` at or before the mark | Evaluated: never runs twice, even after a backward jump |
| `due` in (mark, now], at most 15 min late | Runs once |
| `due` in (mark, now], more than 15 min late | Missed: reported, never run |

So a forward jump, a restart or a long unsynced spell runs only occurrences that are at most 15 minutes late, and a backward jump never repeats a meal.

## Bench plan

Physical work for FW46, none of it motor motion. Each step needs the bench; durations are wall time.

**Network for NTP (static, October 26).** The bench host's firewall (`/etc/computer-security.nft`, table `inet computer_security`, input policy drop) admits from the hotspot interface `wlan1` only DHCP (UDP 67), DNS (TCP and UDP 53) and the broker (TCP 1883 to `10.42.103.1`). UDP 123 from the feeder is dropped, and so would any other port be. No time service runs on the host (`systemd-timesyncd` and `chronyd` inactive), and unprivileged processes cannot bind below 1024 (`ip_unprivileged_port_start` is 1024). So the live test needs the owner to add one input rule beside the hotspot's others, and then either:

- **the standard port:** `iifname "wlan1" ip saddr 10.42.103.0/24 ip daddr 10.42.103.1 udp dport 123 accept`, with the responder run with the privilege to bind port 123 (`sudo`, the owner's call); or
- **an unprivileged port**, which the setting's port allows: `iifname "wlan1" ip saddr 10.42.103.0/24 ip daddr 10.42.103.1 udp dport 1123 accept`, and the feeder set to `--ntp gateway:1123`.

Replies need no rule (the output path is unfiltered and the input chain accepts established flows). The responder serves the host's own clock, which nothing on the host disciplines; that is fine for this check, whose reference is the host.

**1. Live NTP sync (about 45 minutes).** Build and install the `feeder` image over the air. With the hotspot up and the firewall rule in place, run the bounded responder in the foreground: `cargo run -p wellfed-rig --example ntp-serve -- 10.42.103.1:1123 --seconds 2700` (or `:123` with the standard rule). Then `wellfed time --ntp gateway:1123 --zone America/Denver --address <feeder>` and `wellfed time --show`. Pass when, without any client sync since boot: `PLAPI1 ntp server=10.42.103.1:1123`, a `PLTIM1 sync ok=1 source=ntp kind=first` within seconds, the view's `clock_ms` within 1 s of the host and `error_ms` under 100, `offset_s` −21600 or −25200 as the date says, and a `ClockSync` event by the feeder in `wellfed events`. Leave it 45 minutes (three polls): every poll is `used` (October 26's second poll was `bounded` at the old 2,000 ppm), and each poll's `delta_ms` over its spacing is the uptime clock's rate; `learned` stays false with NTP alone; two owner's `wellfed time` syncs about an hour apart turn it true, with `rate_ppb` near the measured rate and `rate_bound_ppb` 1 500 000. Logging those rates over a long run (step 3) shows how much the rate moves within a boot, which is what the learned floor stands for. Then a spoof check: point `--ntp` at a second responder on another port whose host clock is offset (not needed if the responder gains a `--offset-ms`; a member's `TimeSync` an hour off is the simpler check, and must be refused `Time`). Stop the responder and restore the settings (`--ntp gateway`), the hotspot and the firewall as the owner wants them.

**2. The clock across warm resets (about 30 minutes).** Done for the RTC itself (October 26, above). For the carry: build the `feeder` image (its link puts `__sram_end__`, the heap's end, and `wellfed_time_carry` both at `0x1007BFC0`; check the map) and install it. Sync the clock (`wellfed time`), note `wellfed time --show`, then:
- `wellfed reboot` (watchdog): the boot logs `PLTIM1 carry result=resumed gap_s=… error_ms=…` with a gap of the reset's seconds, and the view's `clock_ms` agrees with the host within its `error_ms`, with no sync since boot. This is what decides whether KM4's RAM survives the reset: `empty` or `corrupt` here means the ROM or the reset clears it, and the carry has no home.
- An OTA activation and a trial boot (`wellfed-bench ota`): the same, the record coming from the previous build (a hop through bank 0's recovery role, which does not store a record, shows `resumed` only if the hop took under 10 minutes).
- A power cycle (relay): `result=cold`, the clock invalid, and after the next warm reset `empty`.
- Stop the clock's validity first (`--ntp off`, wait out the window or reboot cold), then `wellfed reboot`: `empty` (an invalid clock is never stored).

**3. Expiry after a long uptime (the carried FW21 item; about 7 hours, unattended, no motion).** Sync the clock once (`wellfed time`), set `--ntp off`, and stop anything that syncs it (the server, the bench's `itest`). After about 6.6 hours the time view's `clock_ms` turns `None` and `Status.clock_ms` with it; a warm reset before then resumes it with the gap added, and a power cycle shows it invalid at once. With NTP on the clock instead stays valid and `error_ms` small, apart from up to a minute around each expiry (the next poll comes within 64 s and re-sets it, `PLTIM1 sync ok=1 source=ntp kind=first`).
